### CHAPTER 4: Unit 4 — Probability, Random Variables & Probability Distributions
* **4.1 Foundations of Probability:** Law of Large Numbers, Complement Rule, Addition Rule (Mutually Exclusive)
* **4.2 Conditional Probability & Independence:** Multiplication Rule, Tree Diagrams, Bayes' Theorem Logic
* **4.3 Discrete vs. Continuous Random Variables:** Probability Mass Functions, Expected Value $E(X) = \mu$, Variance $\sigma^2$
* **4.4 Linear Transformations & Combinations:** Mean & Standard Deviation of $aX + b$ and $X \pm Y$
* **4.5 The Binomial Distribution:** The BINS Framework, Binomial Probability Formula, Mean $\mu = np$, $\sigma = \sqrt{np(1-p)}$
* **4.6 The Geometric Distribution:** The BITS Framework, First Success Probability, Mean $\mu = 1/p$
* **4.7 Calculator Lab:** TI-84 `binompdf`, `binomcdf`, `geometpdf`, `geometcdf`

### CHAPTER 5: Unit 5 — Sampling Distributions & Central Limit Theorem
* **5.1 Parameters vs. Statistics:** Biased vs. Unbiased Estimators, Variability of a Statistic
* **5.2 Sampling Distribution of a Sample Proportion ($\hat{p}$):** Mean $\mu_{\hat{p}} = p$, $\sigma_{\hat{p}} = \sqrt{p(1-p)/n}$, 10% Condition, Large Counts Condition ($np \ge 10, n(1-p) \ge 10$)
* **5.3 Sampling Distribution of a Sample Mean ($\bar{x}$):** Mean $\mu_{\bar{x}} = \mu$, $\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \sigma/\sqrt{n}$, 10% Condition
* **5.4 The Central Limit Theorem (CLT):** When $n \ge 30$, Sampling Distribution of $\bar{x}$ Approaches Normality Regardless of Population Shape
* **5.5 Difference Between Two Proportions ($\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2$) & Two Means ($\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2$):** Normal Approximations
* **5.6 Grader Deduction Traps:** Stating CLT Makes the Original Population Normal (CLT Only Applies to the Sampling Distribution)
* **📝 UNIT 5 PRACTICE WORKBOOK:** 20 MCQs + 3 FRQs with Step-by-Step Annotated Scoring Rubrics

### CHAPTER 6: Unit 6 — Inference for Categorical Data: Proportions
* **6.1 Confidence Intervals for Proportions:** The PANIC Framework, Margin of Error, Critical Values $z^*$
* **6.2 One-Sample $z$-Interval for $p$:** Conditions Check, Formula, Interpretation Frame
* **6.3 One-Sample $z$-Test for $p$:** The PHANTOM Framework, Null ($H_0$) vs. Alternative ($H_a$), $p$-Value Logic, Reject/Fail to Reject Decisions

### CHAPTER 7: Unit 7 — Inference for Quantitative Data: Means
* **7.1 Student's $t$-Distribution:** Degrees of Freedom ($df = n - 1$), Heavier Tails vs. Standard Normal
* **7.2 One-Sample $t$-Interval for $\mu$:** Conditions (Random, 10%, Normal/Large Sample $n \ge 30$), Formula $\bar{x} \pm t^* (s/\sqrt{n})$
* **7.3 One-Sample $t$-Test for $\mu$:** Hypotheses, Test Statistic $t$, $p$-Value Calculation
* **7.4 Matched Pairs $t$-Procedures:** Paired Differences ($d = x_1 - x_2$), When to Use Paired $t$ vs. 2-Sample $t$
* **7.5 Two-Sample $t$-Interval & $t$-Test for $\mu_1 - \mu_2$:** Independent Groups, Conservative $df$ vs. Satterthwaite Approximation
* **7.6 Calculator Lab:** TI-84 `TInterval`, `T-Test`, `2-SampTInt`, `2-SampTTest`
* **7.7 Grader Deduction Traps:** Using Two-Sample $t$-Test on Paired Pre/Post Data Instead of Matched Pairs $t$-Test
* **📝 UNIT 7 PRACTICE WORKBOOK:** 20 MCQs + 3 FRQs with Step-by-Step Annotated Scoring Rubrics

### CHAPTER 8: Unit 8 — Inference for Categorical Data: Chi-Square ($\chi^2$)
* **8.1 The Chi-Square Distribution:** Degrees of Freedom, Right-Skewed Curve, Test Statistic $\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E}$
* **8.2 Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit (GOF) Test:** Single Categorical Variable vs. Hypothesized Distribution, $df = k - 1$
* **8.3 Chi-Square Test for Homogeneity:** Single Categorical Variable Across 2+ Independent Populations/Treatments, $df = (r-1)(c-1)$
* **8.4 Chi-Square Test for Independence:** Two Categorical Variables from 1 Random Sample, $df = (r-1)(c-1)$
* **8.5 Expected Counts Calculation:** $E = \frac{(\text{Row Total})(\text{Column Total})}{\text{Table Total}}$, Large Counts Condition ($E \ge 5$)
* **8.6 Calculator Lab:** TI-84 `$\chi^2$-Test`, `$\chi^2$GOF-Test`, Matrix Operations `[A]` and `[B]`
* **8.7 Grader Deduction Traps:** Checking Observed Counts ($O \ge 5$) Instead of Checking Expected Counts ($E \ge 5$)
* **📝 UNIT 8 PRACTICE WORKBOOK:** 20 MCQs + 3 FRQs with Step-by-Step Annotated Scoring Rubrics

### CHAPTER 9: Unit 9 — Inference for Quantitative Data: Slopes
* **9.1 The Population Regression Model ($\mu_y = \alpha + \beta x$):** Sampling Distribution of Sample Slope $b$
* **9.2 The LINER Conditions Framework:** Linear, Independent (10%), Normal $y$ values, Equal Variance $\sigma$, Random
* **9.3 Confidence Interval for Slope $\beta$:** Formula $b \pm t^* SE_b$, $df = n - 2$
* **9.4 Hypothesis Test for Slope $\beta$:** $H_0: \beta = 0$ vs. $H_a: \beta \ne 0$ (or $>$ or $<$), Test Statistic $t = b / SE_b$
* **9.5 Reading Computer Regression Output:** Identifying Constants, Slopes, Standard Errors, $t$-Ratios, and $p$-Values (Minitab/R/JMP formats)
* **9.6 Calculator Lab:** TI-84 `LinRegTTest`, `LinRegTInt`
* **9.7 Grader Deduction Traps:** Misreading Computer Output Tables (Confusing the Constant Row with the Slope Row)
* **📝 UNIT 9 PRACTICE WORKBOOK:** 20 MCQs + 3 FRQs with Step-by-Step Annotated Scoring Rubrics
